# Journal Discipline

A decision journal is only as good as its worst entry. These rules keep the
entries gradeable and the resolutions honest.

## What makes an entry worth logging

1. **A decision was actually made.** "We are considering" is not a decision.
   Log commitments: what was chosen, by whom, effective when.
2. **The prediction is falsifiable.** Reality must be able to grade it without
   a debate: a number above a threshold by a date, an event happening or not,
   a state observable by a named check. "Adoption will go well" cannot be
   graded. "Weekly active pipelines above 60% of the team by the 90-day
   review" can.
3. **The confidence is a number you would bet at.** 0-100, where 70 means
   "I would take this bet at 7:3 odds". Round numbers that feel safe (80,
   90) are where overconfidence hides; say the number you mean.
4. **The review date is real.** When reality has had time to answer but not
   so long that everyone forgets the context. 30-90 days for most delivery
   calls; quarters for strategy.
5. **The mind-changer is named.** The observation that would flip you before
   the review date. This is the field that turns the journal from a
   scoreboard into an early-warning system — it is the same discipline as
   the devil-advocate's kill criteria, pointed at your own belief.

## Resolution honesty

- **Grade against the prediction as written**, not as you now wish you had
  written it. Moving the goalposts during the grading is how journals rot.
- **HIT** means the prediction held. **MISS** means it did not. **PARTIAL**
  means the substance held and the specifics slipped (the adoption happened,
  the date did not). If you cannot decide between PARTIAL and MISS, it is a
  MISS — that discomfort is the lesson forming.
- **The lesson is forward-looking**: what you would do differently, not a
  summary of what happened. "The plugin spike should have preceded the
  decision" is a lesson. "The migration was harder than expected" is a
  diary entry.
- **Resolve on evidence.** The number, the event, the check. If the evidence
  is not yet in, the entry stays OPEN — a PREMATURE resolution is worse
  than an overdue one, because it poisons the calibration math with noise.

## The overdue debt

An OPEN entry past its review date is listed by the report until resolved.
This is deliberate friction: the journal should nag. When the nag becomes
unbearable, that is the system working — either go get the evidence, or
resolve it with what is known and let the lesson say the evidence was never
collected (that is also a lesson, and an important one).

## What the journal is for

Grading predictions, not people. The moment entries become performance
reviews, the predictions stop being honest — everyone starts predicting safe
things — and the calibration math measures fear instead of accuracy. Keep
the audience to the deciders.
