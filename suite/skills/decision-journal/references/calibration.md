# Reading the Calibration Report

Calibration is the correspondence between what you claim and what happens:
saying "90%" should mean you are right nine times out of ten. The report
measures exactly that gap, per confidence band.

## The bands

- **50-69%** — genuine uncertainty. Getting about half to two-thirds right
  here is *good calibration*, not weakness; these are the calls where the
  evidence was thin and the journal should show it.
- **70-84%** — the working band. Most real decisions live here. A hit rate
  within ten points of the claimed confidence means the confidence numbers
  are trustworthy inputs for the next decision.
- **85-100%** — the fiction zone. Almost everyone overclaims here: studies
  of expert prediction find claimed certainties of 95%+ landing around
  70-85% in reality. If this band's hit rate is 20+ points below its
  average confidence, the fix is structural: stop assigning 95 to anything
  that is not arithmetic or already observed.

## The 15-point verdict

The report flags **overconfident** when average confidence minus hit rate
exceeds 15 points. Why 15 and not 0: calibrated forecasting is a practice,
not a perfection; small gaps are noise, and demanding a 0-point gap produces
the opposite pathology (never committing to a number). Fifteen points is
where the gap starts changing decisions — a decision priced at 85% that
actually lands 65% belongs to a different option class.

## What to do with each finding

- **Overall overconfident**: widen the spread. Say 70 where you used to say
  90, and reserve 90+ for the truly checkable. The decisions themselves do
  not need to change — only the honesty of their pricing.
- **One band overconfident**: localize it. Most people find the fiction
  zone guilty and the working band honest; the fix is then specific — audit
  what earns 85+ and demote the unearned.
- **Underconfident** (hit rate above claimed confidence): rarer and less
  costly, but it means hesitation is being priced as prudence. If 60%
  claims land 85%, the confidences are not informing the decisions anymore.
- **Overdue entries**: debts to reality. Every one of them is either missing
  evidence (go get it) or missing courage (resolve it); the report lists
  them until cleared.

## The long game

The journal's value compounds: after twenty or thirty resolved entries, the
bands stop being statistics and become a personal map — you learn which
*kinds* of calls you price honestly (usually the technical ones) and which
kinds you systematically overprice (usually the ones involving other
people's behavior or your own schedule). That map, not any single
prediction, is the asset: it is how the advisory stack stops repeating its
favorite mistake.
